# Who is the audience?

## Finding

Public evidence supports investigating a substantial Roblox storytelling market. It does **not** establish that the supplied shows primarily reach 16-year-old girls, Los Angeles residents, or even only current Roblox players. We should stop using that persona as the research baseline.

“Gen Alpha / young Gen Z” is a broad business description. Use age bands at the time of research rather than disputed generational boundaries. Distinguish intended audience, actual audience and people incidentally exposed to a clip.

These sources have different countries, dates, recruitment methods and denominators. They are triangulation, not a pooled dataset. No weighted “average viewer” can be computed from them.

## Most defensible segment hypotheses

Segment by viewing behavior first, then compare demographics within and across segments. These segments overlap and have not been sized.

| Candidate segment | What the viewer may be seeking | Observable screening evidence | Main uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roleplay-world enthusiasts | The coherent social experience they imagine when roleplaying | Can describe recent Roblox roleplay viewing or play, a recurring setting and its relationships | Whether playing is necessary to enjoy the story |
| Serialized ensemble fans | Characters to interpret, disagree about and follow | Voluntarily returned to multiple episodes; remembers relationships rather than only thumbnails | Whether they will transfer loyalty to an unfamiliar cast |
| Social-drama clip viewers | An instantly legible disagreement, reversal or awkward exchange | Finds scenes in feeds; can explain why they stopped or left | Whether clip interest produces full-episode viewing |
| Style and identity enthusiasts | Taste, avatar expression and the social meaning of appearance | Describes avatar choices or style-related viewing without being prompted about shopping | Whether fashion matters to the story or merely its look |
| Roblox-adjacent drama viewers | A story worth watching regardless of active play | Watches Roblox stories but seldom or no longer plays | Whether branding the project as “gaming content” excludes them |

The first two are the strongest research leads from the inspected references and comments. That is a prioritization of learning, not a market-share ranking. Style is brand-relevant, but should not get priority merely because Blueberry has fashion assets.

## What we know about the specific shows' viewers

We have qualitative signs of interest in roleplay, character humor, romance, class dynamics and unresolved relationships. We do not have a defensible age distribution, gender distribution, regional split, watch-time distribution or spend profile for any reference channel.

Walden’s protagonist being 16 does not establish that viewers are 16. A comment comparing the show to television does not establish the commenter's age. A feminine avatar does not establish gender. A language choice does not establish residence. A successful title does not establish which ingredient caused viewing.

The supplied shortlist is deliberately success-oriented. It omits many unsuccessful shows, so it cannot by itself reveal which mechanics distinguish hits from ordinary work. Public views also mix exposure, upload age, audience size, packaging, repeat viewing and promotion. Episode-to-episode view differences are not cohort retention.

## Market structure and strategic implication

Think in separate populations: Roblox players → Roblox-content viewers → scripted-drama viewers → returning fans of a specific show → people willing to follow Bumpp. Those populations overlap; they are not a measured conversion funnel. We lack reliable counts at the middle and bottom, so a drama TAM or revenue forecast would be invented.

The competitive set has at least three jobs: provide a world to inhabit, provide a cast to follow, or provide an immediately satisfying feed moment. A single format may not do all three equally well. The observed long episodes make it especially risky to equate “young audience” with “only short attention.” They establish that long-form supply exists and attracts views, not that most viewers finish it.

Working positioning to test: an original ensemble drama in a Roblox-native social world, where characters' choices change their relationships. Do not yet lock school setting, romance emphasis, fashion premise, exact age, protagonist gender, runtime or monetization.

## Demographic questions still open

- Compare 10–12, 13–14, 15–17 and 18–24 separately in appropriately designed research. Do not make the largest band win by combining more ages.
- Include different genders and levels of play. A girl-led cast can be an editorial choice without claiming girls are the measured majority.
- Begin English-language research in the intended launch market; treat UK/global evidence as context, not a substitute. LA remains one possible location, not the audience boundary.
- Separate the age group most interested from the age group for whom a particular story is suitable. Incidental under-13 exposure requires its own assessment.
- Actual audience composition requires creator analytics and/or a properly sampled category study; preference interviews cannot estimate market size.

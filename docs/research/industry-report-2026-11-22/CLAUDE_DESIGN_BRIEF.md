# Claude Design handoff: micro-drama industry intelligence

**Purpose:** turn the accompanying research into an explanatory deck that makes a reader conversant in the industry, the ten selected companies, AI versus conventional production, commercial economics and implications for Blueberry / Bumpp. This is an industry education and decision deck, not a fundraising pitch or a new episode proposal.

## Ready-to-use request for Claude Design

Create a polished 16:9 presentation using the attached `INDUSTRY_REPORT.md`, `chart_data.json`, `SOURCE_REGISTER.md` and `sources.json`. Follow the 36-slide main narrative and 10 appendix topics below. Use speaker notes for supporting detail, definitions, source limitations and discussion questions. The deck must be understandable without this chat.

Preserve the research cut of November 22, 2026 and the fixed Q1 2026 IAP ranking scope. Use exact values from chart_data.json and preserve greater-than/approximately/forecast qualifiers. Every numerical slide needs its source, measurement period, geography and denominator in the footer or visible note. Keep narrative paragraphs in speaker notes rather than shrinking them onto slides. Link source titles to original pages in notes and provide source IDs in footers.

Explain the industry in a logical sequence: what it is → how large and where → who watches → who competes → how content is produced → how distribution and monetization work → what economics and measurement decide → what Bumpp should test. Introduce unfamiliar terms before relying on them. Include a concise conclusion on each company card and one diligence question rather than presenting a wall of facts.

Do not invent data, market shares, margins, CAC/LTV benchmarks, proprietary tools, customer quotes, audience ages, title revenues or experiment results. Use “not disclosed in reviewed evidence” where necessary. Distinguish analyst interpretation from factual evidence. Do not manufacture screenshots, logos, cast imagery, episode frames or quotes; use typography, original diagrams and data visualizations. If external imagery is added, use appropriately licensed material with attribution.

Do not write or generate episodes. The user paused production in favor of audience and market research. Bumpp's proposed audience cohorts and 16–19 target remain company strategy, not measured demographic conclusions.

## Design direction

Use an editorial research style: clean type, strong hierarchy, generous spacing, restrained color, clear tables and purposeful diagrams. Avoid futuristic AI decoration, giant unexplained numbers and generic stock imagery. Pick one consistent accent for sourced current/historical measurements, another for forecasts, and a neutral treatment for proposals and synthetic examples. Do not communicate evidence class through color alone.

Keep the company cards structurally consistent: identity/ownership; dated scale; production evidence; business model; strategic interpretation; open question. Dense supporting records belong in notes or appendix. Use legible footnotes and explicit currency/period labels. All bars start at zero unless a clearly explained alternative is necessary. Forecasts must look different from historical estimates. On non-equally-spaced time series, use a proportional time axis or disconnected cards.

### Numerical and evidence guardrails

- Omdia global-category figures, Deloitte in-app forecasts and Sensor Tower IAP estimates are separate models. Never connect them into one growth curve.
- The broad vertical-video economy is not the micro-drama market. Use D14 only to illustrate scope, not market hype.
- Downloads, users, registrations, active users and payers are different units.
- The ten-app ranking is ordinal Q1 2026 IAP; no bar chart of invented revenue for all ten.
- DramaWave's new-release AI share and FlareFlow's catalog share have different denominators. Never make an exact AI/traditional split from lower bounds.
- Funding, JV capital, app IAP, corporate revenue and gross billings cannot be summed or ranked together.
- An AI sibling does not make the whole parent or another app fully AI-produced.
- Source wordings such as “contains AI” and “AI-powered” have weaker implications than a verified shot-level breakdown.
- HOLYWATER's conversion comparison is observational across different apps; it does not prove production method caused the result.
- The economics model is synthetic and must say so on every slide that uses it.
- Dates must remain attached to facts. An older launch cadence is not today's output; an announced slate is not delivered production.

## Speaker-note pattern

For each slide include: (1) the point in plain language; (2) the supporting facts or reasoning; (3) source links and dates; (4) what the evidence does not establish; and (5) the implication or next question. For company slides, use the full profile section as the note base, paraphrasing rather than copying source material.

## Final quality review

Before delivering the deck:

1. Check every number against its dataset record or the report's linked source, including units, dates, bounds and entity level.
2. Confirm that the ten apps and their ordinal ranks match D08; avoid an unsupported live November ranking.
3. Check that parent funding, portfolio billings and legal-entity revenue are never labeled app IAP.
4. Verify that the AI comparison preserves every limitation and contains no made-up percentage.
5. Check that the audience slide does not turn country-level research into a verified Roblox teenage persona.
6. Recalculate the synthetic model and preserve its label; show no fabricated commercial forecasts.
7. Test reading order, text overflow, footnote legibility and color contrast at presentation size.
8. Make the source appendix and linked notes usable. Include a short methodology/limitations note in the delivered file.

Deliver an editable deck, a PDF if your environment supports faithful export, and a short note listing any chart or layout substitutions. If a chart cannot preserve the required qualifiers, use an annotated table instead.

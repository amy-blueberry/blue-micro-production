# Audience discovery and hypothesis loop

This is a defined research workflow, not an automated approval engine. No participants have been recruited and no findings below are simulated. Current decision: **continue audience discovery; hold episode development**.

## 1. Establish what the existing audiences actually look like

Research lead prepares an analytics request for willing reference creators or partners. It is a request specification, not a message that has been sent.

Ask for aggregate 90-day and 365-day views/watch time by available age bands, gender and country; share of audience represented in each demographic report; new versus returning viewers; long-form versus Shorts; traffic source; episode retention curves; and returning-viewer behavior across releases. Prefer video-specific reporting over channel totals. Record unavailable or suppressed fields, reporting windows and metric definitions.

Account demographics may differ from the person watching; shared family accounts, unknown ages and missing groups limit conclusions. Do not ask for viewer identities, DMs, exact birth dates or personal account access. Do not infer missing under-13 audience from an adult account category. Keep creators' nonpublic aggregates restricted to the agreed purpose.

Compare several channels and less successful uploads, not just the biggest episodes. Record publication date, format, runtime, exposure source and observation date. Raw public view counts cannot isolate story quality. Without access, report “demographic composition unknown”; do not replace it with a confident persona.

**Gate A:** every audience statement is tagged observed, inferred, proposed or unknown. Each numeric claim has a source, population, period and denominator. A failed gate returns to evidence correction.

## 2. Learn viewing behavior before pitching our idea

Research lead and an experienced youth-research facilitator design an initial purposive study: approximately 32 interviews, eight each in ages 10–12, 13–14, 15–17 and 18–24. This is a planning allocation for finding differences, not an estimate of their population shares and not enough to establish statistical superiority.

For minors, use an appropriate professional recruitment process with guardian permission and participant assent, age-appropriate materials, private handling of contact information and the option to stop. The 10–12 workstream requires a separate suitability review; do not show it mature source episodes just because they are Roblox. No unsolicited contact with young commenters. No public age disclosures or rewards tied to watching longer.

Recruit across genders and across active roleplayers, other players and viewers who seldom play. Include existing drama fans and adjacent viewers, recording which is which. These intersecting groups cannot all be estimated with eight people per age band. If the initial research suggests a subgroup difference, expand that comparison rather than announcing a winner.

Start with open prompts:

- What did you last watch all the way through, and how did you find it?
- What made you come back to a series? What made you stop?
- Which character did you change your mind about? What happened?
- Tell me about a funny or uncomfortable moment you remember.
- Do you watch alone, with someone, or while doing something else?
- What makes a Roblox story different from the other things you watch?

Ask about recent concrete behavior before asking what they “usually” like. Do not introduce jealousy, fashion, romance or betrayal until unprompted responses are recorded. Optional brief viewing diaries can help separate recall from actual routines; collect only participant-selected, non-identifying entries, not browsing histories.

**Gate B:** summarize motivations, viewing occasions and disagreements by age and behavior group. Preserve negative cases. If the supposed segment exists only because our questions prompted it, change the segment definition and repeat discovery.

## 3. Compare story territories without producing episodes

After discovery, prepare equally specific, similarly sized descriptions of four distinct territories from H01–H06. Avoid title art, known creator names, brand names and lavish presentation that could determine the result. The descriptions are future research materials, not supplied scripts.

Rotate order. Let participants choose “none.” Ask them to explain the conflict in their own words, choose what they would want to know next, identify whose position interests them and say what feels generic or implausible. Separate “best story” from “best fit for me.” Test one meaningful difference at a time where practical.

Two reviewers independently code a subset of responses, compare disagreements and refine definitions. Do not use an AI impersonation of a teenager as a participant. AI can organize de-identified notes, but a researcher checks every summary against the notes.

**Gate C:** a territory advances as a candidate only if participants can explain its conflict without coaching, describe a specific reason to follow someone, and do not consistently reject the central premise. These are qualitative decision criteria, not validated numeric industry benchmarks. Record both supporting and disconfirming cases. If preferences split, keep separate audience hypotheses rather than averaging them into a bland compromise.

## 4. Measure composition and preference at an appropriate scale

If commercial sizing or an age/gender audience claim is needed, commission a larger category survey from an appropriate sampling frame. Separate category incidence from concept preference. Predetermine geography, eligibility, weighting, quotas, minimum subgroup sizes and uncertainty reporting with the researcher. A convenience poll of followers cannot establish the whole market.

Do not manufacture a sample-size target before defining the difference that matters and the sampling design. A large biased sample still cannot establish population composition. Creator analytics, qualitative interviews and survey results answer different questions and may disagree.

**Gate D:** the final audience brief states a primary behavior segment, evidence-supported age priorities if available, adjacent segments, viewing occasion, emotional motivations, exclusions and unresolved uncertainties. It also names at least one finding that changed our original assumption. Research lead and creative lead document the decision. The current user instruction still holds episode work; passing a research gate does not itself authorize production.

## Defined iterative loop

1. Select the highest-impact unresolved hypothesis in the register.
2. Write the observation that would support it and the observation that would weaken it before collecting new data.
3. Choose the least expensive valid method: source check, analytics, interview, concept comparison or survey.
4. Collect actual evidence under the approved research design.
5. Record sample, exposure, source limitations, contrary findings and interpretation separately.
6. Update status: UNTESTED → EXPLORING → SUPPORTED_FOR_SCOPE / MIXED / NOT_SUPPORTED.
7. Research lead reviews whether the result transfers beyond the studied population. Creative lead translates supported findings into constraints without inventing demographic certainty.
8. Repeat only where unresolved uncertainty affects a decision. If access is unavailable, keep UNKNOWN and choose a limited editorial scope explicitly; do not claim validation.

## Minimal data record

Store: hypothesis ID; source ID; collection date; method; country; coarse age band where appropriately collected; recruitment route; viewing-behavior segment; stimulus/version; exposure order; unprompted response codes; positive/negative/unclear evidence; analyst interpretation; limitations; decision; owner; next action.

Separate participant contact/consent records from research responses. Use study-specific identifiers; do not scrape profiles or infer demographics. Set a research-specific retention/deletion schedule and suppress small identifiable breakdowns before sharing. Store aggregate findings in the creative brief.

## People and deliverables

|---|---|---|
| Audience research lead | Sampling, evidence quality, analytics interpretation and uncertainty | Audience brief and research decision log |
| Youth-research facilitator / safeguarding reviewer | Appropriate recruitment, assent, materials and participant experience | Approved research procedure; suitability decisions |
| Story editor / creative lead | Analyze relationship mechanics and turn findings into distinct hypotheses | Territory comparison and character criteria |

One person may cover several functions if qualified. A production crew, AI-video subscription or finished episode is not needed for this stage.

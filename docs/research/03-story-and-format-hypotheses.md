# Story territories to investigate before choosing a show

These are candidate emotional arcs, not episode outlines or claims about all teens. The evidence references are in the audience and reference-mechanics files. None is validated. Priority means which uncertainty is most useful to test first, not a predicted hit score.

## Candidate arcs

| ID / territory | Relationship movement worth testing | Why it is plausible | What would weaken it |
|---|---|---|---|
| H01: Friendship under change | A secure friendship becomes unequal when one person's opportunities expand; both must decide what they owe each other. | Broad friendship interest in M6; recurring groups and contradictory loyalties in Walden. The specific replacement arc is our invention. | Participants see it as trivial, generic or solvable by one obvious conversation; no interest in either viewpoint. |
| H02: Belonging at a price | An outsider gains access to a desirable group, discovers the conditions and must choose which parts of themselves to conceal or defend. | Walden makes admission and status consequential; Royal Assistant links fitting in with dependency. | Viewers dislike the world enough not to return, or find the status hierarchy unbelievable. |
| H03: Public reputation versus private self | Someone apparently easy to judge behaves differently in private; another character must choose which version to trust. | Walden's differing accounts of Roman and later private context. | The hidden context feels like an excuse for harm, or audiences predict the reversal immediately and lose interest. |
| H04: Recognition versus collaboration | People who need one another's skills disagree about credit, control and who gets seen. | Strings Attached demonstrates competence alongside incompatible wants. Roblox self-expression context makes creation a plausible setting, not a proven preference. | The task feels like workplace administration or brand promotion; viewers care only about the object, not the people. |
| H05: Attraction versus loyalty | New attraction complicates an existing bond; a choice reveals values and changes trust. | Romantic interpretation appears in the Walden sample and its continuing relationships. | Viewers find the romance obligatory, cannot distinguish affection from control, or prefer the friendship relationship without it. |
| H06: Group competition versus solidarity | A shared goal becomes competitive; a fair, understandable rule makes helping a friend personally costly. | User supplied a reality-show reference; currently a lower-evidence territory because its scenes were not analyzed. | Rules feel arbitrary, elimination overshadows attachment, or conflict depends on cruelty rather than competing goals. |

Start research with H01–H04 because they represent meaningfully different motivations and have more direct reference support. Include H05 as a genuine alternative, not an assumption that girl viewers require romance. Treat H06 as exploratory until the actual reality reference is reviewed.

No territory requires the existing jacket, Nia, Kit, Vale or SECOND LOOK setting. Keeping those assets would be a production convenience, not an audience finding.

## Other aspects to hypothesize independently

| ID / variable | Competing possibilities | How to learn without building an episode |
|---|---|---|
| H07: Attachment to characters | Morally clear protagonist versus flawed protagonist with an understandable want | Compare equally detailed character cards; ask who is interesting, who is trusted and who they would follow. These are different outcomes. |
| H08: Source of humor | Relationship history and self-contradiction versus topical language and meme recognition | Ask for remembered funny moments from existing favorites before presenting alternatives. Do not assume slang is either necessary or inherently bad. |
| H09: Desirable world | Familiar social setting versus heightened aspirational setting | Compare neutral setting descriptions with the same relationship problem. Separate “I want to live there” from “I want to watch it.” |
| H10: Style's role | Fashion drives the conflict versus fashion expresses character in the background | Remove brand names and purchase prompts; compare story interest. If interest survives only because of the product, classify it as style content rather than assume drama demand. |
| H11: Audience knowledge | Viewers know a secret before the protagonist versus discover it with them | Compare short premise descriptions; ask what they anticipate and whether uncertainty is enjoyable or confusing. No script required. |
| H12: Continuation reward | A consequential choice versus an unanswered factual mystery | Ask what participants need resolved and what would make them return. Curiosity about a fact may not create attachment to a cast. |
| H13: Viewing occasion and format | Feed discovery, deliberate episode viewing, background watching or social co-viewing | Reconstruct recent viewing sessions and use an optional short diary. Stated runtime preference alone is weak evidence. |
| H14: Roblox specificity | The avatar world is the attraction versus it is an acceptable medium for a good story | Compare explanations from active players, occasional players and viewers who do not play. Do not ask only “Do you like Roblox?” |
| H15: Tone and emotional intensity | Warm ensemble with conflict, heightened melodrama, or comedy with dramatic turns | Have participants describe liked and disliked moments in existing shows, including where tension became unpleasant. Do not optimize distress or compulsive viewing. |

## Character hypotheses, not demographic stereotypes

A character should have a specific want, a useful ability, an imperfect strategy, someone whose opinion matters and a consequence for making a choice. Those are proposed craft criteria. They do not imply that every viewer wants a wealthy school, a cruel popular girl, a shy newcomer or a romance triangle.

Test three separate reactions: recognition (“I know someone like this”), fascination (“I want to understand this person”), and aspiration (“I want something they have”). A viewer need not resemble a protagonist demographically to experience any of them. Do not collapse dislike of a character into dislike of a show.

## What to avoid deciding prematurely

- Exact protagonist age or gender based on an assumed audience majority.
- School, luxury or fashion as mandatory setting ingredients.
- Short episodes merely because the audience uses TikTok.
- A romance requirement, or a blanket prohibition on romance.
- Moral lessons as the organizing purpose of each conflict.
- A universal cliffhanger interval, slang checklist or “viral score.”
- Merchandising plans as evidence that viewers will care about a story.

The output of this stage should be a ranked learning agenda and a supported audience brief. A preferred story territory becomes a development candidate only after the validation gates, not because its table sounds compelling.
